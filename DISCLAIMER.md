Version `c33f179151c4`. This file is written from `backend/app/terms/text.py`; change the words there and run `make terms` to write it again.

This is not legal advice. Have a lawyer read it before you rely on it.

## 1. Test only what you are allowed to test

Blindspot sends real attacks at the targets you give it. Running it against a system without the owner's clear permission can be a crime in your country and in theirs.

You may only point Blindspot at systems that you own, or where the owner has given you written permission that covers the target, the kinds of testing and the dates. Keep that permission. Blindspot asks you to confirm it before any pentest can start, and keeps a record of who confirmed it and when.

## 2. You are responsible for what you run

You decide what is in scope, what may be tried and when. Blindspot's scope checks and limits are safeguards, not a promise that nothing outside your intention can happen, and they do not replace your own care.

Testing can disturb or damage a system, change or delete its data, send mail, lock accounts and cost money. Use a test environment where you can, take backups first, and tell the people who run the target before you begin.

## 3. AI agents make mistakes

Blindspot uses AI models to plan and carry out tests. They can misread a page, invent a result, miss a weakness or choose a step you did not expect. Check every finding before you rely on it or report it, and watch a running pentest.

Text from the models you connect, and from the targets you test, may be sent to the AI providers you configure. Do not test systems whose data you may not share with those providers.
